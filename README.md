## 👨‍💻 About Me

I'm a **Data Scientist and Python developer** with a background in experimental science and a strong interest in machine learning, data analysis, and building useful software around data.

What interests me most is usually not just training a model, but understanding **what is actually in the data, what can reasonably be learned from it, and whether the result will still work once it leaves the dataset it was developed on**.

Most of my projects start with exploring and understanding the data, then move through modelling and validation, and often end up as something people can actually use — a pipeline, dashboard, database, analytical tool, or application.

Python is where I spend most of my time, but I try not to get too attached to a particular model or framework. The problem comes first.

---

## 🔭 What I'm Working On

I'm currently working as a **Data Scientist at IAP in Berlin-Adlershof**, developing data-driven tools for experimental and technical workflows.

My work includes:

* Exploring and modelling complex datasets
* Building regression and classification models
* Investigating model robustness and generalisation
* Feature engineering and multivariate data analysis
* Developing automated data-processing pipelines
* Building databases and structured data workflows
* Creating interactive analytical tools with Python and Streamlit
* Connecting data acquisition, analysis, modelling, and visualisation into reproducible workflows

The data I currently work with comes mainly from scientific and materials-related measurements, but the underlying problems are much broader: **noisy and limited data, correlated variables, domain shifts, validation, automation, and turning analysis into something useful in practice**.

---

## 💡 A Bit About How I Think

I like machine learning, but I'm not particularly interested in throwing twenty models at a dataset and picking whichever gives the highest score.

I'd rather understand **why a model works, where it fails, what information it is actually using, and whether the validation setup reflects the real problem**.

For me, the interesting part of data science sits somewhere between statistics, software, domain knowledge, and a healthy amount of curiosity.
